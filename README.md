# Aviation Accident Analysis

## Introduction and problem statement
This project analyzes aviation accident records for an airline/aircraft insurer. The objective is to identify professionally built airplane makes and models with comparatively low aircraft-destruction rates and low fatal/serious injury fractions when an accident occurs. Records are restricted to 1983 onward, and results are reported separately for small aircraft (fewer than 20 persons aboard) and large aircraft (20 or more).

## Repository contents
- `Aviation_Accidents_Cleaning.ipynb`: data loading, inspection, cleaning, filtering, transformation, and CSV export.
- `Aviation_Accidents_Data_Analysis.ipynb`: EDA, visualizations, confidence intervals, and recommendation tables.
- `data/AviationData.csv`: raw lab dataset.
- `data/AviationData_Cleaned.csv`: generated by the cleaning notebook.
- `data/USState_Codes.csv`: supplied reference data.

## Cleaning methodology
- Keep `Aircraft.Category = Airplane`.
- Exclude amateur-built aircraft.
- Keep events dated 1 January 1983 or later.
- Estimate persons aboard by summing fatal, serious, minor, and uninjured counts.
- Calculate fatal/serious injury fraction as `(fatal + serious) / persons aboard`.
- Derive a binary destroyed-aircraft field from `Aircraft.Damage`.
- Standardize make and model text and combine them into a unique plane-type field.
- Retain makes with at least 50 eligible records.
- Keep original fields having more than 20,000 non-null values, plus the required analysis fields.

## Statistical approach
Make-level comparisons require at least 20 usable records within an aircraft-size group. Plane-type comparisons require at least 10 records. Weather and flight-phase categories require at least 50 records. The analysis includes sample sizes and approximate 95% confidence intervals.

## How to run
1. Place the raw CSV at `data/AviationData.csv`.
2. Run all cells in `Aviation_Accidents_Cleaning.ipynb`.
3. Confirm that `data/AviationData_Cleaned.csv` was created.
4. Run all cells in `Aviation_Accidents_Data_Analysis.ipynb`.
5. Review the displayed make and plane-type shortlists, charts, sample sizes, and confidence intervals.

## Recommendations and observations
The analysis notebook generates the final recommendations directly from the complete dataset. Lower injury and destruction rates are preferable, but recommendations should be limited to groups meeting the sample thresholds and should consider confidence-interval width. Weather condition and phase of flight are analyzed as severity associations. They should not be interpreted as causal relationships.

## Important limitation
This is accident data rather than exposure data. The results describe injury and destruction severity given that an accident was recorded. They do not measure the probability of an accident because fleet size, flight hours, aircraft age, maintenance quality, and operator practices are not fully controlled.
