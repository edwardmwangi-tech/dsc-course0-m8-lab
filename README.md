# Aviation Accident Safety Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes aviation accident records to identify professionally built airplane makes and models associated with comparatively low serious/fatal injury fractions and low aircraft-destruction rates when an accident occurs. Records were restricted to 1983 onward and separated into **small aircraft** with fewer than 20 persons aboard and **large aircraft** with 20 or more persons aboard.

## Data Preparation

The cleaning notebook filters the data to airplanes, removes amateur-built aircraft, standardizes make and model names, calculates persons aboard, creates a serious/fatal injury fraction, and derives a binary aircraft-destroyed indicator. The cleaned dataset contains **17,879 records and 13 columns**. Analysis excludes records with no persons aboard, leaving **17,103 accidents**, comprising **16,225 small-aircraft** and **878 large-aircraft** records.

## Analysis Approach

Aircraft were compared using the mean serious/fatal injury fraction and aircraft-destruction rate. To improve reliability, make-level comparisons required at least 20 usable records, while make-model comparisons required at least 10. Sample sizes and approximate 95% confidence intervals were included. Weather condition and phase of flight were also analyzed as factors potentially associated with injury and damage outcomes.

## Key Findings and Recommendations

- **Small aircraft:** **Cessna 172SP** is the leading make-model recommendation. It had 12 usable observations, a **0% mean serious/fatal injury fraction**, and a **0% destruction rate**, producing a combined score of 0.000. **Diamond Aircraft DA 20 C1** and **Maule M-5-210C** produced similarly favorable results and are suitable alternatives.
- **Large aircraft:** **Bombardier Inc CL-600-2B19** is the leading make-model recommendation. It recorded an approximately **0.41% serious/fatal injury fraction** across 15 observations and a **0% destruction rate** across 11 usable damage observations. **Boeing 777** is a strong alternative for injury outcomes, with an approximately **0.08% serious/fatal injury fraction** across 33 observations, although its destruction rate was 6.25% across 16 usable damage records.
- **Weather condition:** Injury and destruction outcomes differed between visual meteorological conditions and instrument meteorological conditions, indicating that weather conditions may be associated with accident severity.
- **Phase of flight:** Injury and aircraft-damage outcomes also varied across phases of flight, making flight phase another relevant factor when assessing accident severity.

## How to Run

1. Place the raw datasets in the `data` folder.
2. Run all cells in `Aviation_Accidents_Cleaning.ipynb`.
3. Confirm that `data/AviationData_Cleaned.csv` is created.
4. Run all cells in `Aviation_Accidents_Data_Analysis.ipynb`.
5. Review the displayed tables, charts, sample sizes, and confidence intervals.

## Limitation

The dataset contains recorded accidents but does not provide fleet exposure or flight hours. The results therefore describe injury and destruction severity **conditional on an accident occurring** and do not measure the overall probability of an aircraft being involved in an accident. Aircraft age, maintenance, operator practices, mission type, utilization, sample sizes, and confidence-interval widths should also be considered before making underwriting decisions.
